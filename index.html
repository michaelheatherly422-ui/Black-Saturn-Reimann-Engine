1. Riemannian Curvature & The 5D Black Saturn

In standard 4D spacetime, black hole event horizons are restricted to spherical topologies (S²). By extending Bernhard Riemann’s metric tensor (g_μν) into five dimensions (four spatial dimensions plus time), Einstein’s field equations admit the Black Saturn: a central spherical black hole (S³) encircled by a spinning black ring (S¹ × S²).

2. Orthogonal Planes of Rotation (5D to 14D)

In four spatial dimensions (x, y, z, w), rotation occurs in independent 2D planes rather than around a 1D axis. A doubly-spinning Black Saturn rotates simultaneously in the xy-plane and zw-plane without gyroscopic wobble. Scaling to 14 spatial dimensions yields 7 completely orthogonal rotational planes (x₁x₂ through x₁₃x₁₄), mapped here to 7 independent harmonic oscillators and stereo vectors.

3. Rotational Frame Dragging & Dual Extremality

  • Frame Dragging: Even when the central black hole has zero intrinsic angular momentum, the outer black ring churns the surrounding Riemannian metric, inducing sympathetic rotation in the central horizon.
  • Dual Extremality: Distributing angular momentum across orthogonal planes allows both horizons to spin to their theoretical maximum simultaneously, clamping thermal Hawking noise to zero.

4. The 137 (α⁻¹) Fine-Structure Anchor

The 137 Hz / 137.036 Hz anchor is an artistic tuning reference inspired by the numerical value of the inverse fine-structure constant. Because α⁻¹ is dimensionless, it is not itself a frequency. The seven plane ratios and limiter are sound-design mappings, not physical predictions or a simulation of a black hole.

5. Instrument Note

BLACK SATURN uses mathematical and cosmological language as a creative interface for experimental synthesis. Its controls shape oscillators, filtering, delay, stereo placement and dynamics in the browser; they do not reproduce real spacetime curvature, Hawking radiation or higher-dimensional gravity.
